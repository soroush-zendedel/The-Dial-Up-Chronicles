# Project charter

## Mission

The Dial-Up Chronicles is a nocturnal, documentary-style podcast exploring the web's human history through the memories of a former teenager in Zabol and the perspective of an independent engineer and maker today. It connects early online life with curiosity, problem-solving, literature, and the search for belonging.

## Editorial promise

Each episode starts from a vivid human experience, uses technology and historical context to illuminate it, and ends with an open reflection rather than a forced lesson.

## Format and feel

- Calm, reflective solo narration with deliberate pacing.
- Web archives and early online spaces may provide visual texture for video versions.
- The voice stays central. Dial-up, keyboard, and messenger sounds are subtle ambience, not spectacle.
- The project is Persian-first, with a separate English translation for each episode. Other languages may be added later when useful.

## Audience

- People who remember early web communities and want to revisit their intimacy.
- Younger listeners curious about digital history and the perspective of a working developer.
- International listeners reached through English translations and subtitles.

## Place in the Soroush Zendedel channel

The show adds a personal and human-history strand alongside technical web content and problem-solving challenges.

## Pilot premise

A young person in Zabol enters Yahoo chat rooms and learns to read the room, finding a shared language with people who read popular novels. The episode asks how the wish to connect can turn into curiosity and learning.

The working title is **First Connection: Yahoo Chat Rooms and Zabol**. Confirm the intended decade and the role of the reading detail before locking the title or script.
